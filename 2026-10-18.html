What went into the hive this week, and what's coming next.

Published Oct. 18, 2026 · Page look updated Nov. 2, 2026

⚖️ Judges on your ballot

  • New The 57 Judges on Utah’s 2026 Ballot: a case file for every judge Utah voters will be asked to keep or remove, with the Judicial Performance Evaluation Commission’s (JPEC) full 2026 findings. Pick your county to see your judges. JPEC found one judge does not meet its standards (Trent Nelson), and one name on the ballot belongs to a judge who withdrew (Matthew Bell). Seven judges are on every Utah ballot: one Supreme Court justice and six Court of Appeals judges.
  • New How Judges Get on Your Ballot: the plain-words version. Utah voters never pick a judge; they decide whether to keep one. How a judge gets the seat, who grades them before you vote, what the grades mean, and the rules that changed in 2026.
  • New The Hive judge rubric: one grading scale used the same way for all 57 judges, taken from JPEC’s own published wording. A C means the record meets what Utah law requires. No grade is based on how any case came out, or on who appointed the judge.

🗳️ Candidates 2026

  • New How to Research Your Candidates: the take-home guide from our free talk at the Roy Southwest Library on Saturday, Oct. 17. Every free public-records site, step by step, with Weber County examples, the 2026 filing deadlines, all 22 slides with the talk notes, and a printable PDF.
  • New HD-66: Shelley vs. Mashek vs. Hatch (Oct. 13): Juab and Sanpete counties. Each campaign’s money, all three disclosure forms, and Rep. Troy Shelley’s carbon-credit bill next to his own business listings.
  • Updated The Fifth-Fifth Sales Tax and Questions for Incumbents, two reader-submitted documents by Dr. Ronald Mortensen of Citizens for Tax Fairness, are back up, reproduced in full with attribution.

💰 Taxation: twelve years of tax laws, in eight pages

  • New Start here: Tax Cuts and Tax Increases, 2015–2026: every law in our inventory that lowered a tax, raised a tax or fee, or changed who can tax, year by year, and the bills that did both at once.
  • New Utah Tax Cuts: What They Did and All 63 Utah Tax Cut Laws.
  • New Utah’s Tax Changes: 62 Bills in Our Inventory: each bill with its status, fiscal note and sources.
  • New How the Zones Work: TIF, CRA, PID, IFD, MIDA and the Rest: every tool that keeps future property tax growth for a project instead of sending it to the county, schools and districts, and whether voters get a say.
  • New State Laws, Local Taxes: Who Used the Power, and What It Cost You.
  • New Weber County Taxes in 2026: the new sales tax and the property tax increases, and how they compare with the rest of Utah.
  • New The Grand Hyatt Deer Valley: A MIDA-Owned Hotel, Its Tax Bill and Its Lease: the hotel space is owned by MIDA, a state military development authority, and was billed $0 in property tax in 2025 and 2026. The 55 private residences in the same building were billed a median of about $26,700 each in 2026. The page follows the lease, the bonds and the hotel room tax MIDA collects. The $0 bill follows from MIDA’s ownership under state law.
  • Updated The Taxation index, Taxation for Beginners, The Quiet Machine and Cheap on Paper now carry the Utah Hive Politics name and bee.

🔎 Audits: two new, each with a plain-words page

  • New World Trade Center Utah (OLAG 2026-19): the state paid the nonprofit about $2.2 million in fiscal 2025. Auditors found it appears to have done the contracted work and charged trade-mission fees within its contract, but its main result number counts deals that may not happen, and some required reports fell short. In plain words.
  • New iMpact Utah and the MEP money (State Auditor USU25SP): the State Auditor estimated $1.8 million to $2.8 million in manufacturing-program money was used inappropriately, while the agencies passing it along did not check the spending. In plain words.
  • New The GOED audit, in plain words.
  • Updated The audits docket: new layout, dated updates on each case file, and Weber County’s audit committee rules, now marked “adoption not confirmed.”

  • New With Pineview Drawn Down, the Wells That Once Watered Ogden Are Showing Again: Ogden’s pipeline project lowered Pineview Reservoir to some of its lowest levels in decades, uncovering pipes, wood and an old intake from the artesian wells that supplied the city’s water starting around 1914. From reports by KSL, The Ogden Valley News and The Salt Lake Tribune.
