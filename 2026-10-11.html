Issue 03 · Weekly recap

The Sunday Buzz

What went into the hive this week, and what's coming next.

Published Oct. 11, 2026 · Page look updated Nov. 2, 2026
This week was mostly about the two constitutional amendments on your Nov. 3 ballot. Amendments A and B drew more questions and comments than anything we've covered, including public comments from two sitting legislators, so we built a full case file, a myth-vs-fact page, a quiz, and a checked answer for every comment that came in. We also opened a public log of tips, added two pieces to the surveillance series, and profiled the state's economic development corporation. Each box below is one repo or project, and every item is tagged New or Updated.

🗳️ Amendments A and B: the full case file

  • New The Amendments on Your Ballot, and How We Got Here (Oct. 7): both amendments in plain words, the exact bill text, and what a YES or NO vote does. Each side in its own words; charts of the vote thresholds and the votes in the Legislature; how you're told about amendments now and under Amendment A; what voters passed in 2018 (Props 2, 3 and 4) and what the Legislature changed; Amendment D in 2024 and the 2026 court changes; 24 cards on taxes and tax tools the Legislature created; 11 real Utah examples of each tool; a map, who's connected to the record, questions to ask, and words to know.
  • New Fact or Fiction: 20 claims you can test yourself, with a score counter.
  • Updated Comments and responses, checked: more than 20 Facebook comments checked against the record and marked fact, partly true, myth or still open. Commenters are identified by initials only.
  • New Amendment B: Myth vs. Fact (Oct. 6), now with a "Cut and raised" section: every tax the Legislature cut and raised from 2022 to 2026.
  • New Amendment B quiz: "Does Amendment B do what you think?" 12 questions, with your answers compared to what B actually does.
  • Updated Corrections, dated on the page: the YES/NO section now says "tax increase" (as S.J.R. 2 defines it), not "tax change," and the NO column names the 2024 court limit on changing government-reform initiatives. Thank you to the readers who caught both.

Still being added: the money on each side. The No campaign's filings show $897,095 in 2026 cash. No registered committee has reported spending for B, and FOX 13 reports a pro-B mailer from Americans for Prosperity Utah. Those details, plus the legislators' comments word for word, go into the case file next.

🔍 Surveillance: two new pieces, 13 total

  • New What Changed Since September, and What Didn't: Weber County and Utah records since our last update, including the Feb. 17, 2026 Commission consent vote amending the Flock contract, a filterable timeline, charts and a map.
  • New The Questions Residents Keep Asking: eight questions residents ask at council meetings nationwide, what officials said, and what the record shows.
  • Updated Nobody Approves Each Search: newly charged or accused officers in Georgia, Indiana, Kentucky and Florida. All are allegations, not convictions.
  • Updated The Camera Said Match: a new case, Lindsey Isaacs of Palm Coast, Fla., jailed 13 days on plate-reader evidence before another driver was found.
  • Updated A Private Company, Public Power: Flock's 7-day retention default applies to new customers only, according to Flock's Sept. 8 letter to Sen. Josh Hawley.
  • Updated Questions page: the January 2026 Norfolk ruling and the timing of its appeal.

🏛️ Unelected Power

  • New EDCUtah: the Economic Development Corporation of Utah, a public-private nonprofit. Its investors by tier; its board (9 public officials and 15 company seats); its staff and annual reports; the 14 Site Ready Utah sites, including the 891-acre West Weber County Mega Site; a map and a connection diagram. Rep. Jon Hawkins joined EDCUtah's staff in May while serving in the Legislature.
  • Updated Ryan Starks and Utah Rising: now linked to the EDCUtah file.
